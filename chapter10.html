THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 10

The New Student

Tuesday mornings rarely surprised anyone at Jefferson Middle School.

The first bell always rang three minutes early.

Mrs. Collins always carried two cups of coffee.

Mr. Harris always greeted students at the classroom door.

Even the birds gathered on the flagpole before disappearing into the maple trees that lined the parking lot.

Routine gave every day a familiar rhythm.

That rhythm changed when the principal walked into homeroom with a boy nobody recognized.

He stood taller than most seventh graders, wore a faded black hoodie despite the warm weather, and kept his hands buried inside the front pocket as though he expected the entire world to disappoint him.

The principal smiled.

"Class, this is Noah Bennett. His family moved here over the weekend."

A few students waved.

Several returned to their phones.

Noah remained expressionless.

Mrs. Collins pointed toward an empty desk.

"You'll be sitting beside Gabe."

Noah dropped into the chair without looking at anyone.

The morning lessons passed quietly until reading period, when Mrs. Collins divided the class into pairs for a discussion assignment.

"Gabe, you and Noah will work together."

Noah leaned back in his chair.

"I work better alone."

Mrs. Collins smiled.

"Today you'll work together."

He sighed loudly enough for the entire room to hear.

Gabe opened his notebook.

"We only have five questions."

Noah glanced toward the worn Bible resting beside Gabe's backpack.

"You actually read that thing?"

"I do."

"My grandmother used to."

The answer surprised Gabe.

Noah looked away before another word could follow.

Lunch arrived beneath bright skies, and students spilled into the courtyard carrying trays loaded with pizza, fruit cups, and chocolate milk.

Jordan and Hannah waved Gabe toward their usual table.

Noah walked past them.

He chose an empty bench beneath the largest oak tree and stared across the playground while picking at his food.

Tyler watched him for a moment.

"He doesn't seem very friendly."

Gabe followed Noah with his eyes.

"Maybe he's lonely."

Jordan remembered another lunch not long ago when nobody had invited him to sit beside them.

Without speaking, he stood and carried his tray across the courtyard.

Gabe and Hannah followed.

Tyler hesitated before joining them.

Noah looked up with obvious confusion.

"You lost?"

Jordan smiled.

"No."

Gabe sat across from him.

"Mind if we eat here?"

Noah shrugged.

"Doesn't matter."

Conversation moved slowly at first.

Hannah talked about her dog chasing squirrels.

Tyler described accidentally wearing two different shoes to school the previous week.

Jordan laughed so hard that milk nearly came through his nose.

For the first time that day, Noah smiled.

The expression disappeared almost immediately.

"You people always this nice?"

Tyler answered first.

"Not always."

Everyone laughed.

The tension eased.

After lunch the class returned to science, where Mr. Harris announced a partner activity.

Noah folded his arms.

"I'm not doing it."

Mr. Harris remained calm.

"Any particular reason?"

"I've moved four times in three years."

Silence settled across the room.

"I'm tired of pretending people stick around."

Nobody knew how to answer.

Gabe quietly picked up another worksheet and placed it beside Noah.

"You don't have to pretend anything."

Noah stared at the paper.

"What if I mess everything up?"

Gabe remembered the notebook filled with crossed out calculations from the science fair.

"Then we'll figure it out together."

The final bell rang beneath a sky painted with late afternoon sunshine.

Students hurried toward buses while Noah lingered near the front entrance.

He watched Gabe unlock his bicycle.

"Why are you like this?"

Gabe looked puzzled.

"Like what?"

"You keep helping people who haven't earned it."

For several moments Gabe searched for the right words.

Finally he smiled.

"I haven't earned anything Jesus has done for me."

Noah stood perfectly still.

"My dad used to say something like that."

"He sounds wise."

"He died last year."

The sentence landed with quiet force.

Every complaint, every sarcastic remark, and every wall Noah had built suddenly made sense.

Gabe rested one hand on his bicycle.

"I'm sorry."

Noah nodded without speaking.

He climbed onto his bus and disappeared behind tinted windows.

Gabe remained on the sidewalk until the bus turned the corner.

The ride home felt different than every other afternoon.

Jordan had found faith.

Tyler had found humility.

Now another heart carried a different kind of pain.

Some battles were fought against temptation.

Others were fought against grief.

As the evening sun settled behind the trees, Gabe sat on the front porch with his Bible open across his knees.

He prayed for Noah by name.

No dramatic answer interrupted the quiet moment.

No voice echoed from heaven.

Only the steady peace that had guided him through every holy no reminded him that love never begins by changing a person.

Love begins by staying.

For the first time since Noah walked into Room 214, someone had chosen the empty seat beside him instead of the easier one somewhere else.

Gabe smiled as the first stars appeared above the neighborhood.

Sometimes the greatest act of courage was not refusing the wrong path.

Sometimes the greatest act of courage was walking toward the person everyone else had already walked away from.
