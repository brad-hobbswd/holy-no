THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 4

When the Truth Comes Out

Friday morning carried a different feeling.

Students hurried through the front doors of Jefferson Middle School with poster boards balanced against their shoulders, plastic models tucked beneath their arms, and carefully protected projects that represented weeks of work. The annual Innovation Challenge had transformed the gymnasium into a sea of bright colors and nervous excitement.

Tables stretched from one end of the room to the other.

Teachers arranged clipboards.

Judges compared schedules.

Parents volunteered at registration.

Every student hoped today would become unforgettable.

Gabe and Hannah carried their project together, moving carefully through the crowded gym until they found Table Twenty One. Their display looked simple beside the polished presentations surrounding them, yet every graph, observation, and conclusion represented hours of honest effort.

Hannah stepped back and smiled.

“We really built this.”

Gabe looked across the poster board.

Every crooked line reminded him of another afternoon spent testing ideas instead of searching for shortcuts.

“I wouldn’t change a thing.”

Laughter echoed from the opposite side of the gym.

Tyler, Jordan, and Marcus stood proudly beside their display while classmates admired the colorful design.

Jordan caught Gabe looking and lifted his chin with quiet confidence.

Tyler folded his arms.

“You still have time to see what first place looks like.”

Gabe answered with a smile instead of an argument.

Mr. Harris welcomed everyone before introducing three judges from the local university. Each team would explain its experiment and answer questions about research, testing, and conclusions.

The process seemed simple.

Until the questions began.

Several projects received polite applause before the judges reached Tyler’s table.

An older professor adjusted his glasses and studied the display for several moments.

“Excellent presentation.”

Tyler grinned.

“Thank you.”

The professor pointed toward one of the charts.

“Why did your third trial produce different measurements than your first two?”

Tyler blinked.

“It just…did.”

The judge nodded patiently.

“What caused the difference?”

Marcus looked toward Jordan.

Jordan looked toward Tyler.

Silence settled over the table.

The professor turned another page.

“Can you explain why this conclusion contradicts the data collected during the experiment?”

Nobody answered.

Another judge stepped closer.

“Who recorded the observations?”

Jordan swallowed.

“My brother helped.”

The room suddenly felt much quieter.

Teachers exchanged uncertain looks while the judges continued asking questions that should have been easy for students who had completed their own work.

Across the gym, Gabe watched without satisfaction.

He had never prayed for someone else to fail.

His heart felt heavy.

Mr. Harris eventually thanked the boys and invited the next team forward.

“Gabe and Hannah.”

They walked toward the judges carrying nothing except a notebook filled with crossed out numbers, rewritten calculations, and pages covered with mistakes.

The first judge smiled.

“Tell us about your experiment.”

Hannah described their original idea and laughed when she explained how the first model collapsed across the classroom table.

Gabe opened the notebook.

“We had to repeat everything six times because our results kept changing.”

The professor leaned forward.

“What did you learn?”

Gabe turned another page.

“We learned that failure is part of discovering the truth.”

The judges listened carefully while both students explained every correction, every adjustment, and every unexpected problem they had encountered.

Questions continued for nearly fifteen minutes.

Neither child answered perfectly.

Neither child pretended to know something they had not learned.

Honesty carried the conversation from beginning to end.

When the interviews ended, Mr. Harris quietly approached their table.

“I’m proud of both of you.”

Hannah looked surprised.

“We haven’t won anything.”

The teacher smiled.

“Winning is easy when success becomes the only goal. Integrity takes courage that cannot be measured with ribbons.”

Awards were announced an hour later.

Third place belonged to another school.

Second place belonged to Gabe and Hannah.

The gymnasium erupted with applause.

Hannah stared at the stage without moving.

“We actually did it.”

Gabe smiled, yet his eyes wandered toward Tyler, Jordan, and Marcus standing near the back of the room.

None of them looked angry.

None of them looked defeated.

They simply looked disappointed.

As students packed their projects, Jordan slowly walked across the gym and stopped beside Gabe.

“I should have listened.”

Gabe rested the trophy on the table.

“You still can.”

Jordan glanced toward the notebook filled with erased mistakes and unfinished sketches.

“I thought perfect was better.”

Gabe gently closed the cover.

“Jesus has never asked me to be perfect.”

“What does He ask?”

“He asks me to be faithful.”

Jordan stood quietly before extending his hand.

For the first time since the project began, Gabe shook it.

Outside the gymnasium, the afternoon sun broke through scattered clouds and covered the school grounds with brilliant light. Children laughed, parents celebrated, and teachers carried folding chairs back into storage, unaware that something far greater than a science competition had taken place.

One holy no had protected more than a project.

One faithful decision had planted truth inside another heart.

Gabe walked toward home with Hannah beside him, grateful for a second place ribbon that would eventually fade and for a lesson that would remain with him long after every trophy gathered dust.

Some victories are displayed on shelves.

The greatest victories are written where only Jesus and a faithful heart can see them.
