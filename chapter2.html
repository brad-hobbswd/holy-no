THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 2

The First Holy No

Gabe always believed that ordinary days carried the greatest opportunities to honor Jesus. Nothing about that Tuesday morning suggested that a single decision would remain in his memory for years to come. The sun rose over quiet streets, birds gathered along telephone wires, and children climbed onto the familiar yellow school bus with backpacks full of homework and conversations that had nothing to do with faith.

His mother handed him a packed lunch before he walked out the door.

“Have a wonderful day.”

“I will.”

His father looked up from the kitchen table where an open Bible rested beside a cup of coffee.

“Love Jesus more than every decision you make today.”

Gabe smiled.

“That makes every decision easier.”

The words stayed with him during the ride to school and followed him into seventh grade science class, where colorful posters covered the walls and unfinished projects lined the shelves. Mr. Harris stood near the front of the room with the excitement of someone preparing to announce a surprise.

“This year’s Innovation Challenge begins today. You may choose one partner, and the winning project will represent our school at the regional competition.”

The classroom immediately filled with excitement. Chairs scraped across the floor while students hurried toward friends they hoped would help them build the best display in the room.

Before Gabe had time to gather his notebook, Tyler, Marcus, and Jordan surrounded his desk.

“We already know what we’re doing,” Tyler said with confidence. “You have the best grades, so join us.”

Jordan slipped his phone from his pocket and opened a collection of photographs. Every image showed a completed project with detailed charts, polished conclusions, and carefully designed illustrations.

“My brother made this last year,” Jordan explained. “Nobody here has ever seen it, so we can copy the whole thing and change a few details.”

Marcus grinned as though the plan required no further discussion.

“We’ll win without spending every afternoon working.”

Gabe studied the photographs while the voices around him slowly faded into the background. The project looked impressive, and the thought of winning crossed his mind for a brief moment. He imagined bringing home a trophy, making his parents proud, and hearing his classmates celebrate a first place finish.

Another thought quietly replaced every other possibility.

Jesus values truth even when nobody else notices.

He handed the phone back to Jordan without another glance.

“I can’t do that.”

Tyler looked surprised.

“Why not? Nobody will ever know.”

Gabe rested both hands on his desk and answered without anger or hesitation.

“The student who created this would know. You would know. I would know. Jesus would know.”

Marcus laughed.

“You really think God cares about a science project?”

Gabe considered the question before speaking.

“My grandmother tells me that faith grows through ordinary choices. Honesty during something small prepares a heart for something much bigger.”

Jordan folded his arms.

“So you’re saying no.”

“I’m choosing to honor Jesus.”

The three boys exchanged disappointed looks before walking across the room in search of another partner. A few students whispered while others smiled at what they believed was an unnecessary decision, yet Gabe felt an unexpected peace settle over him. He had not gained popularity, but he had protected something far more valuable than approval.

Mr. Harris glanced around the classroom and noticed one student still sitting alone.

“Hannah, would you like to work with Gabe?”

She nodded with a quiet smile and carried her notebook to the empty desk beside him.

“I’ve never won one of these competitions,” she admitted.

Gabe laughed softly.

“Neither have I, so we’re already even.”

The afternoon passed with sketches, ideas, and experiments that failed almost as quickly as they began. Glue stuck to their fingers, pencils rolled onto the floor, and every new design seemed to create another problem that needed solving. Each mistake, however, brought another opportunity to learn, and before long the project started taking shape through patience rather than shortcuts.

As the final bell echoed through the hallways, Hannah gathered her papers and looked at Gabe.

“My dad always says doing the right thing costs something.”

Gabe slipped his Bible into his backpack before answering.

“Following Jesus has never been about choosing the easiest road. Every faithful choice becomes another step closer to Him.”

They walked toward the buses beneath a sky painted with shades of gold and blue while laughter drifted across the parking lot. Tyler and his friends celebrated the project they planned to present, convinced that success belonged to whoever found the quickest path.

Gabe watched them for only a moment before turning toward home. Deep within his heart rested a joy that no trophy could ever produce, and that quiet confidence reminded him that holiness often begins with a single decision made when applause is absent and only Jesus is watching.

The first holy no had not changed the world around him.

The first holy no had changed something within him.
