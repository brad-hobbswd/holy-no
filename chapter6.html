THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 6

The Lunch Table

Tuesday arrived with bright sunshine and the familiar sounds of lockers opening, sneakers squeaking across polished floors, and teachers greeting students by name. Nothing about the morning seemed unusual, yet Gabe had learned that ordinary days often carried extraordinary opportunities.

Jordan walked into homeroom carrying the worn Bible tucked carefully beneath his arm.

He had wrapped the cover in brown paper to protect the frayed edges, and the faded blue ribbon hung from the pages like a quiet reminder that someone had treasured those words long before him.

Gabe noticed the change without saying anything.

During first period, Jordan raised his hand more than usual, volunteered answers without hesitation, and even helped another student solve a difficult math problem. The confidence that had once depended upon impressing people seemed to be settling into something deeper.

By lunchtime the cafeteria buzzed with conversations that bounced from table to table like pinballs. Students traded snacks, laughed over weekend stories, and argued about basketball scores while volunteers hurried between rows carrying trays.

Gabe and Hannah found an empty table near the windows.

Moments later Jordan walked toward them.

His steps slowed.

“So…”

Gabe looked up.

“So?”

Jordan smiled nervously.

“Mind if I sit here?”

Hannah slid her backpack onto the floor.

“There’s plenty of room.”

Jordan lowered himself into the chair and placed the old Bible beside his lunch tray.

Several students noticed.

Tyler noticed first.

He stopped in the middle of the aisle and stared at the table.

Marcus followed his gaze.

“You’ve got to be kidding.”

Jordan calmly opened his milk.

“I’m eating lunch.”

Tyler pointed toward the Bible.

“Since when?”

Jordan rested one hand on the worn cover.

“Since yesterday.”

A few students nearby looked up from their food.

The conversation that followed spread through the cafeteria faster than anyone expected.

“You’ve turned into Gabe.”

Jordan laughed.

“No. I’m trying to learn about Jesus.”

Tyler shook his head.

“You’ve changed.”

Jordan considered the words before answering.

“I hope so.”

The bell ended lunch before anyone said another word, yet the scene lingered in every corner of the school.

By the final class of the day, whispers had already become stories.

Some students claimed Jordan was becoming a preacher.

Others joked that Gabe was building a church inside the cafeteria.

Neither boy defended himself.

Neither boy argued.

Neither boy needed to.

After the last bell, Mr. Harris stopped Gabe as students hurried toward the buses.

“I’ve been teaching for twenty years.”

Gabe waited quietly.

“I’ve watched students influence each other in many ways.”

The teacher smiled.

“Popularity fades faster than chalk on a blackboard. Character leaves marks that remain long after graduation.”

Gabe thanked him and walked outside.

Jordan caught up before he reached the sidewalk.

“I read three chapters last night.”

“What did you think?”

“I kept expecting Jesus to push people away.”

“And?”

“He kept inviting them closer.”

They continued walking beneath rows of maple trees glowing with late afternoon sunlight.

Jordan finally stopped.

“I’ve done plenty of things I wish I could erase.”

Gabe looked toward the open sky before answering.

“My grandmother says grace doesn’t erase a person’s story.”

“What does grace do?”

“It gives Jesus permission to write the next chapter.”

For a long moment neither boy spoke.

Children laughed across the playground while autumn leaves drifted onto the grass, creating a carpet of gold beneath their feet.

Jordan reached into his backpack and removed a folded note.

“I found this inside your Bible.”

Gabe unfolded the paper.

Congratulations. You deserved second place.

Jordan smiled.

“I know who wrote it.”

“Who?”

“I did.”

Gabe looked at him with surprise.

“I couldn’t say those words out loud.”

“You just did.”

Jordan nodded.

“And I’m glad.”

They started walking again, two seventh grade boys carrying backpacks that seemed much lighter than the day before.

Neither understood how far a single decision had already traveled.

One honest choice had created a friendship.

One friendship had opened a Bible.

One Bible had introduced a searching heart to Jesus.

Gabe realized that holiness never ended with saying no to the wrong path.

Holiness always created space for someone else to discover the right one.

As the evening sun settled behind the trees, he smiled and whispered a quiet prayer of gratitude, realizing that the greatest miracles often begin with ordinary faithfulness and continue growing long after anyone notices the first seed breaking through the ground.
