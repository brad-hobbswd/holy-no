THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 8

The Empty Altar

Sunday morning sunlight poured through the stained glass windows of New Life Tabernacle, scattering brilliant colors across polished wooden pews and the worn carpet leading to the altar. Families greeted one another with warm smiles while children hurried toward Sunday school classrooms, filling the sanctuary with joyful conversation before the service began.

Gabe always loved those moments.

Every familiar face reminded him that the church was more than a building. The people gathered inside formed a family connected by something stronger than friendship.

As he reached his usual pew, he noticed Jordan standing near the entrance.

The Bible Gabe had loaned him rested securely beneath one arm.

Jordan looked uncomfortable.

"What if I don't know what to do?"

Gabe smiled.

"You don't have to impress Jesus."

Jordan glanced around the sanctuary.

"What if everyone watches me?"

"They're too busy worshipping."

The worship team began singing, and voices rose together until the room seemed filled with hope. Gabe closed his eyes and quietly thanked Jesus for another opportunity to gather with believers.

Halfway through the service, the pastor stepped behind the pulpit and opened his Bible.

"Today I want to speak about the rich young ruler."

Gabe listened carefully.

The story described a young man who wanted eternal life but struggled to surrender the one thing that held his heart. Jesus never forced him to obey. Love always offered a choice.

Every sentence seemed to settle directly into Jordan's heart.

When the message ended, the pastor stepped away from the pulpit.

"The altar is open."

People began walking forward.

Parents prayed with children.

Grandparents knelt beside teenagers.

Several families gathered together, filling the front of the sanctuary with quiet tears and whispered prayers.

Jordan remained perfectly still.

His hands tightened around the old Bible.

His eyes never left the altar.

Gabe watched without speaking.

Faith could never be pushed.

Love never demanded a performance.

Several moments passed before Jordan quietly shook his head.

"I can't."

The words barely rose above a whisper.

"I've made too many mistakes."

Gabe leaned closer.

"My grandmother tells me that hospitals are filled with sick people, not healthy ones."

Jordan looked at him.

"The altar isn't a place for perfect people. The altar is where broken people meet Jesus."

A tear slipped down Jordan's cheek before he quickly brushed it away.

"I don't know how to pray."

"You already are."

Jordan looked confused.

"How?"

"You've been talking to Jesus ever since you started asking questions."

The pianist continued playing softly while the sanctuary remained wrapped in quiet reverence.

One step.

That was all Jordan needed.

One simple step.

He finally released a slow breath and walked toward the altar.

Nobody applauded.

Nobody turned to watch.

Nobody interrupted the sacred moment unfolding between a searching heart and a loving Savior.

Jordan knelt on the worn carpet, resting both hands on the Bible that had traveled from Gabe's grandfather to Gabe and now to him.

Words refused to come.

Silence filled the space instead.

"Jesus...I want to know You."

Nothing spectacular happened.

The lights remained steady.

The music continued.

The church looked exactly the same.

Jordan stood several minutes later, yet something within him had changed forever.

Peace rested across his face with a calm that had not been there when the service began.

As families gathered in the fellowship hall after church, Tyler approached Gabe with an uncertain expression.

"I saw Jordan praying."

Gabe nodded.

Tyler looked toward the sanctuary doors.

"Do you think Jesus would listen to somebody like me?"

"He listened to me."

The answer lingered long after the conversation ended.

Walking home beneath a brilliant blue sky, Gabe thought about every choice that had led to this morning.

One decision in science class.

One unfinished project.

One honest conversation.

One borrowed Bible.

One invitation.

None of those moments had seemed extraordinary when they happened, yet Jesus had quietly woven them together until they became the story of a heart discovering grace.

Gabe understood something as the church bell echoed across town.

A holy no never exists simply to keep darkness away.

A holy no creates room for someone else to walk into the light.
