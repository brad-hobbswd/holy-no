THE POWER OF A HOLY NO!

Chapter 3

The Cost of Standing

Gabe woke before the alarm sounded and lay quietly beneath the warmth of his blanket while pale morning light slipped through the curtains. Yesterday’s conversation in science class had followed him home, through dinner, and into the quiet moments before sleep. Tyler’s laughter, Jordan’s disappointment, and Marcus’s confident grin replayed in his thoughts, yet another voice remained stronger than all of them.

“I’ll know.”

The words had seemed simple when he spoke them, although they carried far more weight than he realized.

His mother noticed his silence during breakfast.

“Something on your mind?”

Gabe spread strawberry jam across a piece of toast and nodded.

“I keep wondering whether I made the right choice.”

His father folded the newspaper and looked across the table.

“Doing right rarely creates doubt after the decision. Doubt usually arrives before the reward.”

Those words stayed with Gabe long after the school bus disappeared around the final corner.

Students crowded the hallway with the usual energy of another weekday, but conversations seemed to stop whenever he walked past. A few classmates glanced in his direction before whispering to one another.

Tyler stood beside his locker with Marcus and Jordan.

The three boys looked at Gabe for only a moment before turning away.

The distance hurt more than the argument.

He had expected disagreement.

He had not expected silence.

Science class arrived sooner than he hoped.

Mr. Harris clapped his hands together.

“I want every team to give a progress report before the end of class.”

Jordan’s group volunteered first.

Colorful diagrams covered their table, polished charts stood perfectly straight, and every answer sounded rehearsed. Several students leaned forward with admiration while Mr. Harris smiled with obvious approval.

“Outstanding work.”

Tyler glanced toward Gabe as he returned to his seat.

A smile crossed his face.

Not a friendly smile.

The kind that quietly said, You should have joined us.

Gabe looked down at his own project.

Cardboard leaned against a stack of books, handwritten notes filled half the page, and several measurements still refused to make sense.

Nothing appeared impressive.

Nothing looked ready.

Hannah noticed his expression.

“We’re behind.”

Gabe laughed softly.

“I noticed.”

She reached for another sheet of paper.

“My grandfather always says that honest work takes longer because truth refuses shortcuts.”

For the first time that morning, Gabe smiled without forcing it.

They spent the next thirty minutes measuring, cutting, testing, and beginning again every time another mistake appeared. Glue dried in the wrong places, rulers slipped across the table, and one experiment collapsed before either of them finished recording the results.

The classroom filled with laughter.

Tyler looked over his shoulder.

“Maybe you should have copied ours.”

Several students joined the joke.

Heat rose into Gabe’s face, and embarrassment settled heavily across his shoulders. For one brief moment, the easier path seemed impossible to ignore.

Nobody would know.

Nobody except Jesus.

The thought stopped every other argument.

Mr. Harris walked between the tables and paused beside Gabe’s project.

Instead of criticizing the unfinished display, he picked up a notebook filled with crossed out calculations and rewritten observations.

“You’ve repeated this experiment six times.”

Gabe nodded.

“The numbers kept changing.”

The teacher smiled.

“That’s called real science.”

Silence settled across the room.

Mr. Harris placed the notebook back on the table.

“A perfect answer means very little when nobody learns the process. Honest effort creates understanding that copied success never will.”

His words lasted only a few seconds, yet they changed the atmosphere of the classroom.

Several students looked toward Gabe with new respect.

Tyler quietly faced the front.

Jordan stared at the floor.

Hannah leaned close enough for only Gabe to hear.

“You didn’t lose yesterday.”

Gabe looked at the unfinished project spread across the table.

Uneven lines covered the poster board.

Tape showed through one corner.

Pencil marks waited to be erased.

Everything about the display looked ordinary.

Everything about the moment felt extraordinary.

The final bell echoed through the hallway as students gathered their books and hurried toward the buses.

Gabe remained behind long enough to slide his notebook into his backpack.

He understood something now that had never seemed so clear before.

A holy no rarely earns applause on the day someone speaks it.

Time reveals the strength hidden inside obedience.

Walking home beneath bright afternoon skies, Gabe realized that faith was not measured by how loudly a person declared love for Jesus. Faith revealed itself through quiet choices repeated day after day until conviction became part of a person’s character.

The science project remained unfinished.

The friendships around him had changed.

The pressure had only begun.

Yet peace walked beside him with every step, reminding him that no reward offered by the world could ever compare with the joy of knowing he had chosen the right path.
